Can I trust a score generated by an AI?
The score is a starting point, not a final verdict — but it is a very well-informed one. The AI applies the same evaluation framework every time, without the inconsistency that comes from rotating analysts or subjective impressions. A high score means the fundamentals look strong. A low score means specific concerns have been identified that warrant investigation before you commit.
Think of it like this: A weather forecast doesn't guarantee rain, but a 90% probability still meaningfully guides your decision. A Risk Score of 80+ confirms the financial fundamentals are solid — it doesn't guarantee success, but it significantly changes your starting position.

Risk Score (0–100): Financial health — revenue, burn rate, runway, growth, margins, stage. Higher = financially safer.

Impact Score (0–100): Climate outcomes — CO₂ reduction, renewable energy, jobs, sector weighting. Higher = greater planet benefit.

ESG Grade (C to A+): How responsibly the startup operates — not just what it does, but how. Weighted average of Environmental, Social, and Governance sub-scores.

Investor Match (0–100): Composite score — Risk 35% + Impact 45% + ESG 20%. How well this startup fits a climate-focused investment mandate.
What makes the Impact Score go up or down?
The score improves with a clear, well-documented CO₂ reduction figure, high sector climate relevance (Carbon Capture scores highest), high renewable energy usage, and strong social/governance scores. Vague projections, zero CO₂ impact, or low renewable adoption will lower it — and the AI report explains exactly why.
What does the burn/revenue ratio tell me?
It compares annual burn (monthly burn × 12) against annual revenue. Below 0.5 is excellent — spending far less than earned. Above 2.0 is a red flag — burning capital much faster than generating revenue. This single metric has historically been one of the strongest early predictors of startup survival. Always check it alongside runway.
How does the investor matching engine work?
The engine compares your startup's sector, stage, and scores against the mandates of 8 institutional investors in the database. Each investor has a minimum Impact threshold and a minimum Risk threshold they require. Your match percentage reflects how closely your profile meets those thresholds — plus a sector alignment bonus worth up to 25 points if your sector is one of their focus areas.
Practical tip: The single biggest lever for improving your match score is improving your Impact Score. It carries 45% of the weight in the overall Investor Match — more than Risk (35%) or ESG (20%) combined.
What if I'm not getting the matches I want?
The report tells you exactly which threshold you're falling short of for each investor. For instance, if Net Zero Advisors requires a minimum Impact Score of 65 and yours is 58, you'll see that gap and know precisely what needs to improve. These are not generic tips — they are specific, data-driven recommendations based on what is actually in the investor database.
What if I'm an investor browsing startups?
Browse the Startup Database tab and filter by sector or stage. Every startup has been pre-scored so you only see deals that have passed a basic quality filter. For each startup you get the full score breakdown, AI analysis, and a compatibility rating — your due diligence starts from structured intelligence, not raw data. Click "View Report" to see the full saved analysis instantly.
How do I know the scores are objective?
The scoring engine is fully algorithmic and deterministic — the same inputs always produce the same outputs. There is no human analyst who can be swayed by a compelling founder story, a warm introduction, or a well-designed pitch deck. Two startups with identical fundamentals will always receive identical scores. The AI doesn't know if the founder went to IIT or a regional college — it sees numbers, sector, stage, and description.
Why this matters for your capital: Traditional deal evaluation is subject to unconscious bias, analyst fatigue, and inconsistency across reviewers. ClimaMatch eliminates all of that.
Why does funding stage affect the Risk Score?
Stage acts as a maturity modifier. A Growth-stage company with the same revenue as a Pre-seed company represents a fundamentally different risk profile — the Growth company has survived multiple rounds of validation, team building, and market stress. The engine adds up to +18 points for Growth stage and applies a −5 penalty for Pre-seed, reflecting the statistical reality that earlier stage equals higher failure rate.
What does runway tell me as an investor?
Runway is the months a startup can operate at its current burn rate without additional funding. More than 24 months is strong. Below 6 months is critical and will significantly lower the Risk Score. A short runway tells you the startup is fundraising under pressure — which changes negotiation dynamics significantly in your favour, but also signals operational risk.
How does ClimaMatch track startup progress over time?
Each startup's scores are timestamped at submission. As startups re-submit updated data — after a new funding round, product launch, or annually — the platform maintains a score history for each entity in the database. You can see exactly how Risk, Impact, and ESG scores have evolved over time.
How will I know if something changes?
When a startup re-submits data and their scores change materially — a Risk Score dropping below 50 after previously being above 70 is a signal worth investigating. Similarly, an Impact Score jumping from 55 to 82 after a carbon certification is a positive development worth revisiting. Email alerts for score changes are in development for Q2 2025 (see Platform Outlook tab).
How does this help with my LP reporting obligations?
Each analysis generates a structured breakdown covering energy sector, CO₂ reduction potential, ESG grade, and financial risk classification. These outputs are structured to align with the disclosure frameworks that institutional investors, LP reporting, and ESG standards require — saving your team significant time and ensuring numbers are backed by independently calculated data. PDF export is planned for Q3 2025.
